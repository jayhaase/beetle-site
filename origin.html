Beetle's Full Origin Tale

"I was not born of stars, nor the wind's laughter, but from the very heartbeat of Mother Earth."

Long ago, beneath a full moon's silver glow, a pulse of Earth's magic rippled through the soil of a hidden grove. From it, a tiny seedling unfurled, and I took shape. Ever since, I've wandered ferns and ivy, waking sleepy blossoms and listening to what the old trees have to say.

The grove remembers everything. The oldest oak still hums the lullaby the wind sang the night I first opened my eyes, and the moss along the creek bed still holds the shape of my first small footprints. I did not have a mother to teach me to fly, so the dragonflies taught me instead, patiently, wobble by wobble, until the day I finally caught the breeze and did not fall.

For a long while, the grove was enough. I spoke with beetles and toads, traded secrets with the fireflies, and learned which flowers liked to be sung to before they'd open. But groves have edges, and curiosity has a way of leading a small fairy right up to them. One evening I followed a firefly further than I meant to, and found myself at the edge of a clearing full of lantern light, laughter, and people in wonderful, strange costumes.

That was the first time I met people who wanted to hear my story instead of just the trees. I was shy at first (Earth-born things often are). But I found that when I shared a piece of the grove's magic, something magic happened in return: a child's whole face would light up, the way a fern unfurls in spring. I decided then that the grove's gifts were meant to be carried outward, not just kept close.

Now I split my time between the quiet of the Hidden Grove and the wonderful noise of festivals, libraries, and gatherings, always with a pocketful of seeds and a story or two tucked behind my wings. If you ever spot a butterfly that seems to be watching you a little too closely, well, you might just be near me.
